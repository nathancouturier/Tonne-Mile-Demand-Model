1. Question: how much of a move in the Baltic Dry Index is cargo volume, how much is average haul, how much is fleet growth and how much is port congestion, and what is left over?
2. Method: a log-difference attribution identity, log(BDI1/BDI0) = e[dlog volume + dlog haul] minus e[dlog fleet + dlog at-sea share] + residual, with one elasticity e estimated on twelve-month changes (Newey-West errors) and checked on levels with month dummies, quarterly four-quarter changes, sample halves and an unrestricted first-difference regression; tonne-miles built route by route from customs data and a maritime routing network (271 routes).
3. Finding: e is about 2.4 (twelve-month changes, R squared 0.07, 154 months of data); the physical forces leave about 91% of a typical twelve-month move unexplained and fleet growth is a steady drag; adding Brent and the lagged iron ore price as market signals cuts the unexplained share to 55% in sample and 62% out of sample, a commodity-cycle effect the customs volumes miss; in the latest window the largest physical driver was fleet growth.
4. Limitation: speed and anchorage waiting are not observable from free data and sit in the residual; the price signals are market correlates, not causes, and were chosen on the same sample; adding the minor bulks or OECD leading indicators did not raise the out-of-sample R squared by more than 0.05; the public monthly index ends earlier than the demand data; representative ports and customs timing add measurement error.
5. Next: replace the price signals with physical cycle measures (China steel output, monthly ship deliveries, berth waiting times), add a Capesize versus Panamax split if sub-index history becomes available, and estimate a time-varying elasticity to test whether the market's sensitivity to utilisation has changed since 2021.
