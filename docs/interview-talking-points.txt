1. Question: how much of a move in the Baltic Dry Index is cargo volume, how much is average haul, how much is fleet growth and how much is port congestion, and what is left over?
2. Method: a log-difference attribution identity, log(BDI1/BDI0) = e[dlog volume + dlog haul] minus e[dlog fleet + dlog at-sea share] + residual, with one elasticity e estimated on twelve-month changes (Newey-West errors) and checked on levels with month dummies, quarterly four-quarter changes, sample halves and an unrestricted first-difference regression; tonne-miles built route by route from customs data and a maritime routing network (271 routes).
3. Finding: e is about 2.4 (twelve-month changes, R squared 0.07, 154 months of data); the four forces give the direction of 66% of twelve-month moves, fleet growth is a steady drag, and the residual averages 0.39 log points against typical moves of 0.43; in the latest window the largest measured driver was fleet growth.
4. Limitation: speed, waiting time and bunker costs are not observable from free data and sit in the residual; the route set covers major bulks only; the public monthly index ends earlier than the demand data; representative ports and customs timing add measurement error.
5. Next: add minor bulks and a Capesize versus Panamax split from the Baltic sub-indices, bring in AIS-derived speed and congestion series, and estimate a time-varying elasticity to test whether the market's sensitivity to utilisation has changed since 2021.
