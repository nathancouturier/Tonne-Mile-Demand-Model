Tonne-Mile Demand Model (Python, D3, GitHub Actions): built an automated, fully documented pipeline that decomposes moves in the Baltic Dry Index into cargo volume, haul distance, fleet growth and port-time effects using only public data (UN Comtrade, Brazil Comex Stat, IMF PortWatch AIS data, UNCTADstat, NGA World Port Index), with sea distances computed on a maritime routing network for 271 major bulk routes, extended to an annual decomposition from 1980 with a calibrated Kilian-index proxy for the freight index before 2011.
Estimated a freight rate elasticity of 2.4 to the tonne-mile over effective fleet balance (twelve-month changes, 154 months of data); the physical balance alone leaves about 91% of a typical twelve-month move unexplained, and two public price signals (Brent crude, iron ore) cut this to 55% in sample and 62% out of sample.
Published the results as an interactive research-note website with weekly data refresh, validation and cross-source reconciliation checks, and a generated PDF note.
